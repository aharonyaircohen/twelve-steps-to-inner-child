The **12-Step Program**, as presented in the sources, is a structured journey of spiritual and psychological recovery designed to address the "spiritual disease" of addiction and the survival patterns developed in **dysfunctional homes**. It is described as a "neurological exercise" intended to dismantle the ego and replace survival-based defenses with presence and connection.

Below is an extensive context regarding the program, its mechanics, and its core concepts.

### 1. The Foundational Base (Steps 1–3)
The first three steps are considered the "base" (פונדמנט) that supports the entire structure of the subsequent steps.

*   **Step 1 (Admitting Powerlessness):** This step involves recognizing that one’s life has become unmanageable and admitting powerlessness (חוסר אונים) over the patterns learned for survival. It requires **honesty** to see how compulsive behaviors have taken over.
*   **Step 2 (Openness to a Higher Power):** Recovery begins when an individual is open (פתיחות) to believing that a Power greater than themselves (כוח גדול/עליון) can restore them to sanity. In this context, "sanity" often means moving away from a life dictated by childhood trauma.
*   **Step 3 (The Decision to Hand Over):** This is a **decision** (החלטה) to turn one’s will and life over to the care of God as understood by the individual. The sources define "handing over" as putting one's will into a "locker" or under "supervision" (השגחה) rather than asking God to fulfill personal desires.

### 2. The Internal Inventory (Steps 4–5)
These steps move from theory to intense, written action to clear the internal "slate."

*   **Step 4 (Searching and Fearless Inventory):** Participants write down a detailed "moral inventory" to move things from the unconscious to the conscious. This is done using specific **tables** to list **resentments (טינות), fears (פחדים), and secrets (סודות)**. The goal is to "identify without identifying"—to see the patterns (like "people-pleasing" or "rebellion") without letting them define who you are.
*   **Step 5 (Admission and Ego Collapse):** Admitting the exact nature of one's wrongs to God, oneself, and another human being. The primary goal here is to **collapse the ego**. By being completely honest with another person, the "defensive walls" of the ego have no more secrets to hide behind, which breaks the isolation and shame typical of dysfunctional upbringings.

### 3. Core Concepts and Terminology
*   **The Inner Child (ילד פנימי):** The authentic self that was hidden or "disconnected" due to an environment where it felt unsafe to "talk, feel, or trust". Recovery involves becoming a **loving parent** to this child.
*   **The "False Personality" (אישיות כוזבת):** A survival identity (e.g., being a "hero," "victim," or "rebel") built to cope with a home that didn't function.
*   **Resentment (Resent-ment):** Defined as "re-feeling" (להרגיש שוב ושוב) old pain. It is described as a poison that keeps the individual stuck in the past, replaying the "trauma film" instead of living in the present.
*   **The Obsession Triangle:** A cycle consisting of **Resentment** (past), **Anger** (present), and **Fear** (future).
*   **The Drama Triangle (Karpman):** The toxic roles of **Victim (קורבן), Rescuer (מציל), and Persecutor (תוקף)** that keep individuals trapped in repetitive, painful relationship dynamics.

### 4. Key References and Literature
The program draws from various historical, psychological, and spiritual references:
*   **Bill W. (William Wilson):** A co-founder of Alcoholics Anonymous (AA).
*   **William James:** His work, *The Varieties of Religious Experience*, is cited regarding the "spiritual collapse" of the ego.
*   **Carl Jung:** Quoted on the importance of making the unconscious conscious to avoid being controlled by "fate".
*   **S. Stephen Karpman:** Referred to for his "Drama Triangle" model of social interaction.
*   **Step Literature:** Constant references are made to the **"Big Book"** and **NA (Narcotics Anonymous) literature** for step definitions and prayers, such as the **Serenity Prayer** (תפילת השלווה).

### 5. Essential Tools for Recovery
*   **Sponsorship (ספונסר):** A mentor within the program who has completed the steps and guides others through the process.
*   **The Group:** Essential for breaking the silence of a dysfunctional background.
*   **The "Just for Today" (רק להיום) Concept:** A tool focusing on staying in the present moment rather than being overwhelmed by the past or future.
*   **Kavanah (Intention) vs. Control:** Moving from trying to control outcomes (Self-Will) to focusing on actions and leaving results to a Higher Power.
