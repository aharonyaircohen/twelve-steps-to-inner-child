Here is the full list of organizations, literature, and key figures referenced in the program. Please note that while the **names** of these resources appear in the sources, the specific **URLs** are provided from external knowledge to help you find them online.

### **1. Primary Organizations and Fellowships**
*   **Adult Children of Alcoholics & Dysfunctional Families (ACA):** This is the primary framework for the course. It focuses on the "14 traits of adult children" (The Laundry List).
    *   *Reference:* [adultchildren.org](https://adultchildren.org)
*   **Alcoholics Anonymous (AA):** Cited as the origin of the 12-step method and the "Big Book". It covers the story of co-founder Bill W..
    *   *Reference:* [aa.org](https://aa.org)
*   **Narcotics Anonymous (NA):** Referenced for its specific literature regarding Step 3, Step 11, and the "Obsession Triangle" (Resentment, Anger, Fear).
    *   *Reference:* [na.org](https://na.org)
*   **Sexaholics Anonymous (SA):** Mentioned regarding Tony A.'s influence on alternative step-work for survivors.
    *   *Reference:* [sa.org](https://sa.org)

### **2. Foundational Literature and Books**
*   **"The Big Book" (Alcoholics Anonymous):** The core text of AA, specifically cited for the "Actor/Director" metaphor.
*   **"The Varieties of Religious Experience" by William James:** This book is identified as a critical influence on the program's understanding of the "ego collapse" required for a spiritual experience.
    *   *Reference:* Available for free at [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/621).
*   **"Why Zebras Don't Get Ulcers":** A book title and metaphor used to explain the difference between a physical stress response and a toxic mental narrative.
*   **Tony A.’s 12 Steps:** An alternative version of the 12 steps specifically for Adult Children, focusing on the internalization of parents' traits.
*   **The Laundry List:** The 14 traits of an adult child, used as a diagnostic tool.

### **3. Key Figures and Philosophers**
*   **Carl Jung:** Cited for his concepts of the **Unconscious** (making it conscious to avoid "fate") and the dynamic between the **Shadow** and the **Mask**.
*   **Sigmund Freud:** Referenced regarding the tripartite model of the psyche—**Id, Ego, and Super-Ego**—and how Step 5 forces these parts into honesty.
*   **Stephen Karpman:** Developer of the **"Drama Triangle"** (Karpman's Triangle), used to explain the roles of **Victim, Rescuer, and Persecutor**.
    *   *Reference:* [karpmandramatriangle.com](https://karpmandramatriangle.com)
*   **Janet G. Woititz (referred to as "Jenny"):** A pioneering psychotherapist and researcher on **Adult Children of Alcoholics** and codependency.
*   **Bill W. (William Wilson):** Co-founder of AA whose recovery story is a foundational lesson in the program.
*   **Tony A. (Tony Agnello):** A major figure in the history of ACA who shifted the steps toward a focus on childhood trauma.

### **4. Program Tools and Concepts**
*   **The HOW Principles:** The three attitudes required for the program: **Honesty (כנות), Openness (פתיחות), and Willingness (נכונות)**.
*   **Serenity Prayer (תפילת השלווה):** A grounding tool used at the start of meetings to differentiate between things that can be changed and things that must be accepted.
*   **Just for Today (רק להיום):** A primary tool for staying focused on a manageable 24-hour window.
*   **Identify without Identifying (לזהות ולא להזדהות):** A mantra used to observe character defects without letting them define your identity.
*   **The Three Rules:** "Don't talk, don't feel, don't trust," identified as the toxic rules of a dysfunctional home that the program seeks to break.
